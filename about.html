THE COMPANY

Built by hand, staged anywhere

Body Art Cabaret started as a handful of performers who wanted to build a show around the room instead of shipping the same set to every venue. That habit never left — it just grew a tour bus.

HOW IT STARTED

A troupe with no fixed address

Body Art Cabaret began the way most touring shows do — a small group of performers, a shared rehearsal space, and more ambition than budget. What set the company apart from the start was a refusal to treat the show as a fixed product. Every room is a different shape, a different crowd, a different night, so the company builds toward the room rather than asking the room to fit the show.

That approach turned out to be the whole identity. Costumes are cut in-house, choreography is reworked between stops, and the crew that hangs the rigging is the same crew standing in the wings during the number. Nothing about the production is outsourced or templated.

"We don't tour a show. We tour a company, and the company builds a show wherever it lands."

THE PROCESS

How a night comes together

Every stop on the route follows the same sequence, whether it's a 90-seat black box or a festival main stage.

Read the room

The company scouts the venue's size, sightlines, and rigging points before a single number is set.

Build the set list

Acts are pulled from the rotating repertoire and reordered to fit the crowd, the stage depth, and the night's pacing.

Rehearse on-site

A full technical and blocking rehearsal happens in the actual venue, not a stand-in studio.

Perform & strike

The show goes up, the room gets one night built specifically for it, and the company loads out for the next stop.

WHAT GUESTS CAN EXPECT

The standards behind the glamour

Made, Not Bought

Costumes, props, and set pieces are designed and built by the company, not sourced off the rack.

Safety First, Always

Aerial and rigging work follows strict inspection and spotting protocols at every single venue.

Consent on Stage

Every act is built around clear boundaries — for performers and for the audience they invite in.

Built for the Room

No two stops get the identical show — the set list flexes to the venue, the crowd, and the occasion.
